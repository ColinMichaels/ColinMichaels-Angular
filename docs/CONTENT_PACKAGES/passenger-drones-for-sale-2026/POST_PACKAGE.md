### Passenger Drones for Sale in 2026? I Checked the Real Order Pages

The internet calls them passenger drones because “single-occupant piloted ultralight electric vertical-takeoff aircraft” is a terrible thumbnail.

The nickname is useful. It is also where the confusion starts.

Some of the machines in those viral videos are real enough to order. The Jetson ONE has a live checkout path and an estimated 2028 delivery. Pivotal still advertises a refundable Helix reservation. RYSE announced RECON reservations in 2023, but I could not verify a current purchase path on its first-party website when I checked in August 2026.

That is not a three-way buying recommendation. It is a status check on a young category where “for sale,” “reserved,” “delivered,” and “flew once in a video” are four different claims.

I have not flown, owned, ordered, or received any of these aircraft. Nobody covered the research or paid for placement. I checked current manufacturer pages, dated company announcements, the eCFR text for Part 103, and an independent RYSE flight video on August 14, 2026. Prices, slots, and delivery language can change; re-check the linked pages before sending money.

**Embed:** Robb Report, *The Ryse Recon Is an Ultra-Light eVTOL for One Person. We Took It for a Ride* — `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=95idHg7jGrs`

This independent 2023 flight is evidence that a RECON prototype was flown by a journalist. It is not evidence that a current 2026 retail order will be accepted or delivered.

### TLDR

- Jetson’s current order page lists the ONE at $148,000 before taxes and fees, with an $8,000 non-refundable downpayment and estimated 2028 delivery.
- Pivotal’s current site lists the Helix from $190,000 and a fully refundable $9,000 reservation before a $50,000 production deposit.
- Pivotal’s page still says the reservation secures a 2026 delivery position. Because that language is time-sensitive in August 2026, confirm the actual slot in writing rather than treating the page copy as a promise.
- RYSE opened RECON reservations in January 2023 and described a late-2023 production target. I could not verify a current first-party order page in August 2026.
- These are single-seat piloted aircraft—not autonomous passenger-carrying drones—and the manufacturers position the U.S. versions around Part 103 ultralight rules.
- Part 103 removes several certification requirements, but it limits the vehicle to one occupant and sport or recreation, and restricts where and when it can fly.
- My verdict: Jetson and Pivotal show real purchase mechanisms; neither is a practical city commuter, and RYSE requires fresh first-party proof before consideration.

### Jetson ONE: The Clearest Active Order—and the Longest Wait

Jetson has the cleanest answer to “Can I place an order?” Its current order page lists a $148,000 full price before taxes and other fees, an $8,000 non-refundable downpayment due now, and estimated 2028 delivery. The page says 2026 and 2027 production are sold out.

The company also announced its first global delivery in September 2025, to Palmer Luckey in California. That is a meaningful milestone because delivery is stronger evidence than a reservation counter. It is still one company-announced delivery, not proof of mass production or an ordinary buyer’s delivery time.

Jetson’s detailed product page lists eight electric motors, an approximate 20-minute flight time, a software-limited 102 km/h or 63 mph top level speed, a 210-pound maximum pilot weight, and a 253-pound mass including batteries. The company says no pilot’s license is required in the United States and identifies the U.S. vehicle as a Part 103 ultralight.

The catch is larger than the price. An $8,000 non-refundable payment today buys a distant production position in a category where hardware, company schedules, training, regulations, insurance, support, and the buyer’s own circumstances can all change before 2028.

**My status:** genuinely orderable, visually the closest match to what people mean by “passenger drone,” and far too speculative for an impulse deposit.

### Pivotal Helix: More Enclosed, More Expensive, More Structured

The Pivotal Helix looks less like a scaled-up camera drone and more like a tiny personal aircraft because it is a fixed-rotor tilt-aircraft with tandem wings and an enclosed cockpit.

Pivotal’s current personal page lists a $190,000 starting price. It describes a fully refundable $9,000 reservation, followed by a $50,000 production deposit, final payment, training, and delivery or pickup. That staged process is clearer than a vague “join the future” form.

It also contains a date problem worth noticing: the page says the reservation secures a 2026 delivery position. On an August 2026 check, that promise needs a conversation, a written delivery estimate, current terms, and a refund review before money moves.

Pivotal lists a 220-pound payload, 55-knot or 62-mph cruise speed, 20-minute maximum endurance, 20-mile maximum range, a 14-by-14-foot assembled footprint, and a 75-minute charge on a 240-volt charger. The company also advertises triple-redundant controls, automated takeoff and landing, an aircraft parachute, emergency floats, and required training.

Those are manufacturer specifications and safety claims, not my test results. They also do not change Part 103’s operating limits.

**My status:** the most structured active U.S. reservation and training story here, with a price and use case that still make it specialist recreational aviation.

### RYSE RECON: The Useful Warning About Old Headlines

RYSE opened RECON reservations in January 2023 and said it expected production deliveries later that year. A separate August 2023 company announcement described RYSE as pre-revenue while also saying the aircraft was available for purchase in the United States.

The RECON did fly. Robb Report’s aviation editor piloted one in a 2023 video, and the company published test and demonstration milestones. That matters as prototype evidence.

What I could not verify in August 2026 was a functioning current first-party product and order path with current price, deposit, terms, and delivery timing. The company domain returned an error during this research check, while the easy-to-find reservation story remained the 2023 announcement.

That does not prove RYSE is gone, that no private sales conversation exists, or that nobody ever received an aircraft. It means the public evidence available to a buyer did not clear the same bar as the active Jetson and Pivotal pages.

**My status:** interesting aircraft, real historic flight evidence, no recommendation until RYSE supplies fresh first-party purchase and delivery proof.

### They Are Not Passenger Drones in the Part 107 Sense

Part 107 governs small **unmanned** aircraft. These vehicles put one pilot in the aircraft. The manufacturers are instead using the U.S. Part 103 ultralight route.

Under the current eCFR text, a Part 103 ultralight vehicle is for a single occupant and sport or recreation only. A powered ultralight must weigh less than 254 pounds empty, excluding specified floats and deployable safety devices; it also has speed and stall limits. The operator does not need an airman or medical certificate under Part 103, and the vehicle does not need aircraft registration or an airworthiness certificate under that part.

That sounds freeing because it is. It is not permission to use one like a car.

Part 103 generally restricts operation to daylight, prohibits flying over a congested city, town, settlement, or open-air assembly, and requires prior ATC authorization in Class A, B, C, or D airspace and certain surface Class E airspace. The operator must see and avoid other aircraft. Local property, launch, noise, environmental, and insurance constraints can still matter.

### Before Anyone Sends a Deposit

1. Confirm the exact aircraft configuration qualifies for the legal category the seller describes in the place you intend to fly it.
2. Get the current total price, deposit refundability, production position, delivery window, training, freight, taxes, warranty, spare-parts, battery, and cancellation terms in writing.
3. Ask for customer delivery evidence, not only reservation counts, prototype flights, or influencer footage.
4. Map the actual operating area against airspace, congested-area, property, daylight, and local restrictions.
5. Price storage, a trailer or transport system, charging, battery replacement, maintenance, insurance, and a safe practice area.
6. Review the company’s current financial and production risk with a qualified adviser before making a large or non-refundable payment.
7. Treat a viral driveway or coastline flight as a production—not as a template for an ordinary legal flight.

Use the [Personal Aircraft Buyer Verification guide and printable worksheet](https://colinmichaels.com/resources/personal-aircraft-buyer-verification) to capture the offer, deposit terms, claimed legal category, operating reality, support answers, and evidence before treating any checkout page as a purchase decision. ### My Verdict

Yes, you can place money behind a personal multicopter in 2026. Jetson presents a real $148,000 order page and a 2028 estimate. Pivotal presents a real Helix reservation path from $190,000. That makes this category more than vaporware.

It does not make the category mature.

The short endurance, single-seat limit, rural operating reality, training, transport, ownership cost, long production timeline, and non-refundable risk turn these into specialist recreational aircraft for unusually prepared buyers. Pivotal’s refundable first step is materially less aggressive than Jetson’s non-refundable one, but neither should be entered from a thumbnail-induced adrenaline spike.

RYSE is the cleanest reminder that a launch announcement ages. Until a current first-party order path and delivery evidence are easy to verify, I would watch the RECON and keep my wallet closed.

The future is real enough to have a checkout page. It is not yet simple enough to call transportation.
