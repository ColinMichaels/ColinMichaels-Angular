## Blog Draft

### The $1,100 Laundry Chair Solves a Real Problem—But Is It Worth It?

There is a category of clothing that defeats both the closet and the hamper: worn once, not exactly clean, not dirty enough to wash. It usually ends up on a chair until the chair disappears.

Simone Giertz built furniture around that confession. Her Laundry Chair is a real upholstered chair with a movable wooden rail for hanging those in-between clothes. Rotate the rail behind the chair and the visual mess retreats with it. The seat stays a seat.

As of August 15, 2026, Yetch Studio lists the Laundry Chair as a **$1,100 preorder** in green or red, with worldwide shipping and an estimated November 2026 ship date. That makes it a more interesting question than whether the idea is clever. The idea is obviously clever. The question is whether it is $1,100 clever.

I have not ordered, assembled, sat in, loaded, or tested a Laundry Chair. This is a research-based product and preorder analysis, not a hands-on review or buying recommendation. Nobody paid for placement, supplied a chair, or provided an affiliate link. I checked Yetch Studio's current product page and policies, Kickstarter's creator interview, Simone Giertz's official video, and current U.S. consumer guidance on August 15, 2026. Re-check the live pages before spending money.

**Embed:** Simone Giertz, *The Laundry Chair is finally here!* — `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F3rI7IVoBp4`

The video is useful evidence of the design, mechanism, prototype, and creator's intended use. It is not an independent comfort, durability, assembly, delivery, or long-term storage test.

### TLDR

- The Laundry Chair combines a corduroy lounge chair with a movable hardwood rail for once-worn clothing.
- The rail can rotate behind the chair, hiding hanging garments while leaving the seat usable.
- Yetch currently lists it at $1,100 before shipping, in green or red, with worldwide delivery and an estimated November 2026 ship date.
- The listed chair is about 36.7 × 36.4 × 36.7 inches, weighs about 45 pounds, supports up to 440 pounds, and is claimed to hold at least 10 garments depending on size and type.
- It arrives flat-packed, includes tools and hardware, has a removable cushion cover, and carries a listed two-year warranty.
- The preorder is not the same as a finished chair ready to ship: Yetch says packaging design and bulk material purchasing remain, the red color is still being refined, and the delivery estimate can change.
- My verdict: it is a sharp piece of functional furniture for someone who loves the object and already needs a chair. It is hard to justify as storage alone.

### Four Numbers That Explain the Chair

- Current preorder price: **$1,100**, before shipping and possible import charges.
- Footprint: **36.7 × 36.4 inches**, roughly a three-foot square before allowing room to move around it.
- Listed weight: **45 pounds** assembled.
- Claimed capacity: **at least 10 garments**, depending on their size and type.

Those are Yetch Studio's specifications and capacity claim, not my measurements. The same page lists a 440-pound seat limit and a 55-pound shipping box.

### The Product Starts With an Honest Behavior

The best thing about the Laundry Chair is that it does not demand a new habit before it becomes useful. People already drape once-worn jeans, shirts, and sweaters over a bedroom chair. The product notices that behavior and gives it structure.

The rail matters more than the chair. Hanging keeps garments separated and visible instead of compressing everything into an archaeological layer cake. Rotating the rail behind the seat preserves the room's calmer face without pretending those clothes went back into the closet.

Kickstarter's March 2026 creator interview says the project began after Giertz noticed the proportions of a guest-room chair and started sketching. It also describes the Laundry Chair campaign as the source of one of Kickstarter's most viral social posts. That response makes sense: the invention turns a private mess into a shared joke, then offers an actual mechanism instead of a lecture about being tidier.

### What the $1,100 Buys

Yetch lists a solid hardwood frame, 100% cotton corduroy upholstery, reinforced seams, a concealed cushion zipper, and a ball-bearing rotating mechanism for the rail. The cushion cover is removable for washing. The chair ships flat-packed with instructions, tools, and hardware.

That is meaningfully more substantial than bolting a coat hook to a discount chair. It is also furniture pricing, not gadget pricing. You are paying for an upholstered lounge chair, a large hardwood structure, a low-volume first production run, and a recognizable creator's product design.

The buying test is therefore not, “Could hooks hold my clothes?” Of course they could. It is, “Would I choose this as my chair even if the rail were not a viral joke?” If the answer is no, the storage feature has to carry too much of the price.

### The Preorder Is the Real Catch

Yetch estimates shipping in November 2026 and says later production batches may have later dates. Its FAQ also says the design is finished and a factory has made multiple versions, while packaging design and bulk material purchasing still remain. The company explicitly allows for production delays.

The red upholstery is still being refined, so its final production shade may change. Shipping is calculated at checkout. Customs or import fees may apply outside the seller's included charges, and Yetch says those costs are the buyer's responsibility.

Yetch's current support policy says an order can be changed or canceled before shipment through the confirmation-email link. After delivery, its general return policy gives 14 days for an item in original condition with original packaging; international return shipping is normally the customer's responsibility unless the product is faulty. Confirm that these general terms apply to this specific preorder before paying, especially because returning a 55-pound furniture box is not a casual errand.

For U.S. online orders, Federal Trade Commission consumer guidance says sellers must ship within the promised time and, if shipping is delayed, give the buyer a choice between accepting the delay and canceling for a full refund. That is a useful backstop, not a replacement for reading the seller's current terms, keeping the receipt, and asking questions before the charge.

**Preorder boundary:** November 2026 is an estimate, not proof of a completed production run or a chair ready to leave a warehouse today.

### Before Preordering One

1. Tape a 36.7 × 36.4-inch rectangle on the intended floor and check door, closet, bed, and walking clearance.
2. Decide whether you genuinely need another lounge chair, not only a better clothes pile.
3. Try a seven-day “wear again” zone using a rack, valet, or hooks you already have.
4. Confirm the final color, delivered price, shipping charge, taxes, customs, cancellation path, return shipping, warranty coverage, and replacement-parts process in writing.
5. Save the product page, estimated ship date, receipt, and seller messages.
6. If delivery timing matters, wait for finished production units and independent owner reports.
7. Do not treat a viral video or polished prototype as long-term comfort and durability evidence.

### My Verdict

The Laundry Chair succeeds as design because the joke disappears into the function. The rail is not decorative storytelling pasted onto a normal chair. It directly manages the garment limbo that created the project, and rotating it behind the seat is the satisfying part.

At $1,100, though, it should be judged first as furniture. I would want to sit in a production unit, rotate a fully loaded rail, assemble the flat pack, remove and replace the cushion cover, and see how the mechanism feels after months—not minutes—before calling it a value.

If you already want a distinctive upholstered chair, love Giertz's design work, have the floor space, and accept a first-production preorder, the rail could make this unusually personal furniture rather than an absurdly expensive rack.

If the goal is simply to stop burying a chair under jeans, buy or improvise the “wear again” zone first. The Laundry Chair is the most charming answer. It is not the only useful one.
