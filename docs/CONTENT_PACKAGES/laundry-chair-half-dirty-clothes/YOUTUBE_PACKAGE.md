## Six-Minute Research Video Script

### 0:00–0:22 — Cold Open

[Open on a normal chair disappearing under jeans and shirts. Cut to Colin on camera.]

Every bedroom has a chair that stopped being furniture and became a waiting room for clothes. Not clean enough for the closet. Not dirty enough for the hamper. Simone Giertz turned that exact failure into a real product—and the preorder price is $1,100.

### 0:22–0:43 — Disclosure and Promise

[On-screen label: RESEARCHED — NOT HANDS-ON]

I have not ordered, assembled, sat in, or tested this chair, and this is not sponsored. I checked the current Yetch product page, policies, Kickstarter interview, and Simone's launch video on August 15. Here is what the chair solves, what the money buys, and the preorder catch I would settle before paying.

### 0:43–1:28 — The Mechanism

[Use the article's generic editorial cover and a simple animated rail path. Link or embed the official video; use excerpts only after rights review.]

The Laundry Chair is an upholstered lounge chair with a wooden rail for those once-worn clothes. Hang them instead of building a pile. Then rotate the rail behind the chair so the clothes are still separated but the room looks calmer.

That last move is the product. A coat rack stores clothes. This turns the storage out of sight while keeping the seat open. It does not fight the habit. It gives the habit better furniture.

### 1:28–2:08 — Four Numbers

[Full-screen evidence card, source label: Yetch Studio, checked Aug. 15, 2026.]

The current preorder is $1,100 before shipping. The chair is about 36.7 by 36.4 by 36.7 inches and weighs about 45 pounds. Yetch says the rail can hold at least 10 garments, depending on what they are, and the seat is rated to 440 pounds.

Those are manufacturer numbers, not my measurements. Tape that footprint on your bedroom floor before the price makes the whole decision for you.

### 2:08–2:53 — What the Price Buys

[B-roll labels: hardwood frame; cotton corduroy; rotating bearing mechanism; flat-pack assembly; removable cushion cover.]

Yetch lists a solid hardwood frame, 100% cotton corduroy, reinforced seams, a concealed cushion zipper, and a ball-bearing mechanism for the rail. It ships flat-packed with the tools and hardware and has a listed two-year warranty.

So this is not a $1,100 hook. It is a low-volume upholstered chair with an integrated storage mechanism. But it still has to win as a chair. If you would not want the seat without the viral idea, the rail is being asked to justify too much money.

### 2:53–3:33 — The Cheap Test

[Show the package's problem-comparison and alternatives illustrations.]

Before ordering one, create a “wear again” zone for seven days. Use a valet, a rack, over-door hooks, or one hook beside the chair you already own. If the clothes still land on the floor, the problem may not be missing furniture.

If the simple test works but you hate how it looks, that is where Giertz's design becomes genuinely interesting. It combines the behavior, the seat, and the visual cleanup in one object.

### 3:33–4:25 — The Preorder Catch

[On-screen label: ESTIMATED NOVEMBER 2026 — NOT READY TO SHIP]

Yetch currently estimates November 2026 shipping and says later batches may ship later. The FAQ says the design is finished and the factory has made multiple versions, but packaging design and bulk material purchasing still remain. The final red shade is also being refined, and the company says production hiccups can move the date.

Shipping is calculated at checkout, customs may apply, and a returned furniture box is listed at about 55 pounds. The current general policy says you can cancel before shipment and gives 14 days after delivery for an unused return in original packaging. Confirm the exact preorder terms in writing before paying.

### 4:25–5:18 — Who It Is For

[Three concise cards.]

Best fit: someone who already wants a distinctive lounge chair, loves Simone's design work, has roughly a three-foot-square footprint, and accepts the uncertainty of a first production run.

Bad fit: someone looking only for the cheapest way to stop a clothes pile, a renter with tight floor space, or anyone who needs furniture by a fixed date.

Wait-and-see fit: someone who loves the mechanism but wants independent reports on comfort, assembly, rail feel, fabric wear, delivery, and warranty service from finished production units.

### 5:18–5:58 — Verdict

[Colin on camera; show the chair-pile opening again.]

The Laundry Chair is a good design because the joke and the function are the same thing. The rotating rail directly solves the behavior that made it viral.

At $1,100, I would judge it as furniture first and clever storage second. If I already wanted this chair, the rail could make it special. If I only wanted my jeans off the chair, I would start with a hook.

### 5:58–6:15 — Comment and Continuation

Where do your once-worn clothes go right now: chair, hook, closet, or hamper? Tell me the honest answer. The full dated preorder checklist and sources are linked below. Then watch the next “Is It Actually Useful?” episode on the end screen.
